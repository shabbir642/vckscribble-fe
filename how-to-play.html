How to play Drawdle

One person draws, everyone else guesses. Here is everything else, from starting a room and playing in teams to drawing a word nobody should be able to draw.

Start a game

  1. Open drawdle.in, type your name and pick an avatar.
  2. Create a room. You are the host.
  3. Share the invite link, or read out the room code for people to type in.
  4. Choose your settings while people arrive, then press Start.

Players can join a game that has already started; they are added to the turn order.

A turn

The drawer gets a few words to choose from and picks one in secret. Then the clock starts: the drawer draws, and everyone else sees the drawing appear live, with a blank for each letter of the word.

As the time runs down, letters are revealed one by one. The turn ends when the time is up or everyone has guessed it, and the word is shown to all. Everyone draws once per round.

Guessing

Type your guess in the chat and press Enter. Guess as often as you like. Capitals, accents and punctuation don't matter.

A guess that is nearly right gets a private “is close!” so you know to keep going. Once you have it, your guesses stay hidden from the players still guessing, so you can't give it away.

Scoring

  • A right guess scores more the sooner it comes, with a bonus for the first few players to get it.
  • The drawer earns points for each player who guesses right.
  • After the last round, the highest score wins and lands on the podium.

Play in teams

The rules above are for everyone playing for themselves, as a new room does. In a team game one of you draws, the rest of your team guesses, and the other teams watch.

Setting up

  • In the lobby, the host switches “Everyone for themselves” to “Teams” and picks 2, 3 or 4 teams: Team Mango and Team Grape, then Team Watermelon and Team Kiwi.
  • Each player is put on whichever team is smallest, and so is anyone who joins later. To switch, tap Join on another team. The host can also shuffle the teams.
  • The host, or anyone on a team, can rename it.
  • In a big room the lobby suggests how many teams to have. Every team needs at least 2 players before the game can start.

Turns, guesses and points

  • The teams take turns to draw, and in a round everyone draws once. If the teams are uneven, the smaller ones draw extra turns, so every team gets the same number of turns.
  • The drawer picks one of the words offered: in a team game nobody can write their own.
  • Only the drawer's team guesses. When a guess is close, the whole team sees it, so you can work it out together.
  • The first right answer wins the turn and the points for the team, and the sooner it comes, the more it scores. If the time runs out first, nobody scores.
  • After the last round, the results show the winning team.

When another team draws

You get the word too, covered until you tap “Show word”. Watch the guesses come in, react to the drawing and chat with everyone else watching: the drawing team can't see that chat. Just don't say the word out loud.

What the host can change

  • Teams: everyone for themselves, or 2, 3 or 4 teams.
  • Rounds: 1 to 10.
  • Draw time: 15 seconds to 4 minutes.
  • Max players: 2 to 30. A new room allows 30.
  • Word choices: 1 to 5 words for the drawer to pick from.
  • Hints: how many letters get revealed, from none to five.
  • Word packs and difficulty: fourteen packs, in Easy, Mixed or Hard.
  • Custom words: up to 500 of your own, mixed in or on their own (at least 10). When everyone plays for themselves, you can also let drawers write their own word on their turn.

The host can remove a player. Anyone else can vote to kick, and a majority of the room decides; in a team game, that has to include a vote from the player's own team.

Draw better

  • Start with the big shape. An outline in the first five seconds beats a perfect detail at the end.
  • Draw the scene, not just the thing. Stuck on “umpire”? Draw the stumps and a raised finger.
  • Split long words. “Pressure cooker”: draw the pot, then the whistle and the steam.
  • Use arrows and circles to point at what matters.
  • No letters or numbers. Writing the word spoils the fun, and everyone will tell you so.

Guess better

  • Count the blanks. Two short gaps means two short words.
  • Guess early and often. Wrong guesses cost nothing, and a near miss tells you you're close.
  • Think like the pack. With Desi on, that triangle is probably a samosa.

That's all there is. The rest you learn by losing.
